Recruitment Channel Performance Analytics

The business questionWhich recruitment channels deliver suitable hires at a reasonable acquisition cost, and which keep providing value once retention and employee success are considered?

Open the live report ↗Opens in a new tab. No sign-in needed.

About the report

An interactive Power BI report that follows hiring from application to retained employee. It combines six recruitment KPIs (conversion rate, cost per hire, recruiter hours per hire, time to fill, retention and successful hire rate) and ranks every channel with a weighted score that executives can re-weight to match their own priorities.

What each page answers

  1. Recruitment ActivityHow many applicants become hires, and where they drop out
  2. Cost and Time InvestmentWhat each channel costs in money, recruiter hours and days
  3. Quality and OutcomesWho is still employed and successful after 90 and 180 days
  4. Strategic Channel EvaluationA weighted score and a recommended action for every channel

Key findings

  • Employee referrals rank first with a weighted score of 98/100, against 74/100 for the next best channel, direct sourcing.
  • Referrals beat the overall average on all six KPIs, from conversion (10.75% vs 2.87%) to 180-day retention (89.6% vs 79.5%).
  • Only 2.87% of applicants become hires. The biggest drop-off is 75.3% at qualification, while offer acceptance is strong at 81.63%.
  • Agency hires cost €11,339 each, nearly five times the €2,367 average, and agencies take 59.17% of total spend. €640.85K of spend (6.69%) went to campaigns that produced no hires.
  • Direct sourcing is a strong quality-focused alternative, careers sites are the lowest-cost option at €735 per hire, and job boards are the leading source of hiring volume.
  • Social media brings 41.38% of applicants but scores lowest at 34/100, with only 62.23% of hires retained at 180 days.

Business implications

Established a performance-based framework for choosing recruitment channels. Decision makers can prioritise employee referrals and pick alternative channels to suit a specific goal: cost control, candidate volume or hire quality.

Project 2 / Customer support · 12,000 tickets · 2025–2026

AI Customer Support Analytics

The business questionHow can support leaders see where AI delivers the most operational value, when a human is needed, and how routing decisions change the customer experience?

Open the live report ↗Opens in a new tab. No sign-in needed.

About the report

A three-page Power BI report that compares three ways of resolving a ticket: AI only, AI handing over to a human, and human only. Each route is measured on six metrics: initial resolution rate, seven-day repeat rate, SLA achievement, cost per ticket, resolution time and customer satisfaction. The final page turns the comparison into a recommended route for every issue type.

What each page answers

  1. Demand and Customer ExperienceTicket volume, waiting times and satisfaction by channel, customer tier and handoff
  2. Support Model EfficiencyResolution, repeat, SLA and cost for each support route and each issue
  3. Issue Routing RecommendationsThe recommended route, the evidence and the estimated saving for each issue

Key findings

  • AI only is the strongest operational approach across most issue categories in the dataset.
  • For account lockouts, AI only resolves 96.31% of tickets first time, with a 9.20% seven-day repeat rate, 100% SLA achievement, €1.11 per ticket, 0.05 hours to resolve and 4.30/5 satisfaction.
  • AI to human is the weakest route. It records the lowest satisfaction on the demand page, and for account lockouts it scores 3.55, below the 4.0 satisfaction gate, so it is not recommended.
  • Billing disputes are the hardest issue: 35.10% repeat rate, 24.59 hours to resolve, only 49.08% of tickets within SLA and €39.85 per ticket. Integration errors cost the most at €48.78.
  • Overall, 95.01% of 12,000 tickets were resolved first time, total support cost was €162.72K (€13.56 per ticket) and average resolution took 6.04 hours.

Business implications

Developed issue-specific routing recommendations that help support leaders balance efficiency, service quality and customer experience. They show where AI-only handling adds value and where another support route deserves consideration.

Key findings

  • London generated the highest regional revenue (£5.09M) but the lowest net profit margin of the 12 regions, at 7.38%.
  • Rent was the real determinant of profitability.
  • Loyalty members contribute 33.19% of total revenue, while redemption costs are only 2.38% of total costs. For every £1 spent on loyalty, £17.21 of revenue is generated.
  • Adoption is highest at transport hubs (45.75%) against 32.83% on the high street, and 32 of 60 stores are above average on both adoption and revenue contribution.
  • Espresso drinks lead the product mix at 45.6% of revenue, and Thursday, Monday and Wednesday are the strongest weekdays with 46.5% of revenue between them.

Business implications

Recommended keeping the loyalty programme while monitoring incremental customer spending to establish its true return. Recommended shortlisting expansion regions with a composite performance score rather than relying on revenue alone.
